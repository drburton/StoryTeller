# Code of Conduct

Last updated: 2026-10-11

StoryTeller is made by and for writers, artists, students, teachers, and programmers with very different levels of experience. This code of conduct keeps the project a place where all of them can ask questions, share work, and disagree about ideas safely.

It applies in every project space: the repository's issues, pull requests, discussions, and reviews, and anywhere someone represents the project, such as a talk or a class that teaches StoryTeller.

## What we expect

- Be welcoming and patient, especially with newcomers and with people writing in a second language.
- Critique code, designs, and writing. Leave people's character, background, and ability out of it.
- Accept that decisions sometimes go against you, and say so respectfully when you disagree.
- Give credit for other people's work and ideas.
- Respect people's time: search before asking, and keep reports clear and specific.
- Follow the originality rules in `CONTRIBUTING.md`, and respect other people's copyrights and trademarks.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, threats, intimidation, or encouraging someone to harm themselves.
- Insults, slurs, or demeaning comments about anyone's identity, body, age, disability, ethnicity, nationality, religion, sexual orientation, gender, or gender identity.
- Unwelcome sexual attention, or sexual content in project spaces.
- Sharing someone's private information, such as an address or email, without their permission.
- Repeatedly derailing discussions, or continuing to contact someone after they asked you to stop.
- Posting content that the project could not legally publish, such as pirated assets or another product's source code.

Stories and art made with StoryTeller can cover mature subjects. Discussing a game's content for a technical question is fine; keep explicit material out of project spaces and use descriptions or links with a content warning instead.

## Reporting a problem

If someone's behavior troubles you, contact the maintainer privately through the contact details on https://github.com/drburton. Include what happened, where (links help), and who was involved. Reports are kept confidential, and only the people needed to handle them will see them.

You can also use GitHub's **Report content** option on any comment, issue, or pull request to reach GitHub's own staff.

## What happens next

The maintainers review every report and decide on a response that fits what happened. Responses range from a private note or a request to edit a comment, through a temporary ban from project spaces, to a permanent ban. Maintainers who are part of a report step aside from handling it.

People who report in good faith will not face any penalty for it.

## Questions about this code

Open an issue if something here is unclear or could be better.
